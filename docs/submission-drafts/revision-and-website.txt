Thank you for the feedback on AviScope. This revision addresses it as follows.

What changed
- Revenue change in the summary: the latest filed revenue is compared with the immediately preceding period of the same scope, currency and unit, with the absolute and percentage change and a citation for both filed figures. If no comparable period exists, the summary gives the reason instead of a percentage, and a group figure is never used in place of the company's own. On the 100-company smoke test, {{smoke_revenue_compared}} companies were compared and {{smoke_revenue_not_compared}} were not.
- Websites now need proof on the page: a site is published only when the fetched page itself shows the company's exact legal name or organisation number. A domain that merely spells the name no longer counts. Pages that name another legal entity, such as a parent, group, sibling or client list, are withheld. This addresses the two group/parent sites you found. I did not hard-code the companies you named.
- Social profiles are published from links on a confirmed company site and from JSON-LD sameAs, one per platform, when the handle matches the company. Facebook's newer page links (facebook.com/p/Name-id) are now recognised. Platform pages are never fetched. On this sample, {{smoke_company_profiles}} companies have a linked profile.
- Runs now always finish: results are written as soon as each company finishes, the run keeps a deadline margin for writing output, and every input gets exactly one result, marked failed or partial if needed, even when a source stalls. Local tests cover 100, 300, 1,000 and 1,100 inputs. A live run on 300 companies from my own held-out sample (not your 300) returned {{extra_terminal}} results for {{extra_input}} inputs ({{extra_failed}} failed) in {{extra_runtime}} s, using {{extra_requests}} requests at ${{extra_cost}}. I could not find the cause of the 108-of-300 result from my side, so I am sending a short separate question about it.
- Public website built and hosted with JBox: {{website_url}} . It covers search, company profiles, evidence receipts, comparison and methodology, with a 3D research-lens illustration that has Pause and Reset controls.

What did not change: news and NAV jobs. The free GDELT news API rate-limited 11 of 12 polite test queries (HTTP 429), and the licensed feeds I found are paid. So I did not add a news source rather than loosen the fetch policy. NAV's job feed needs a registered production token, which I have not requested yet. Jobs and articles still come only from structured data on confirmed company sites, and anything not found stays "not established".
