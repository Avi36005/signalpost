# Exact entity publication

Official records are addressed by the input organisation number. Registry and annual-account response identifiers must equal that number. Subunit `overordnetEnhet` must equal it; the subunit's own number stays separate. Company roles are scoped to the public exact-entity role endpoint. Group responses remain group-scoped claims and never populate subsidiary financial metrics.

External website publication uses ordered evidence tiers. **November 2026 rule:** every tier needs the fetched page itself to show the exact legal name or the organisation number. A registry-listed or name-shaped host is a ranking hint only, never ownership proof on its own. Every tier also requires: no other checksum-valid organisation number on the page, no parked-domain marker, and a host that is not a known shared portal or site builder (`SHARED_HOSTS`, e.g. OBOS/USBL housing portals, shopping-centre sites, Wix/Squarespace subdomains, directories).

1. **Exact organisation number on the page.** Registry-declared domains accept it directly. A name-derived (guessed) domain also needs the domain to spell the legal name or the page to show the exact legal name. The number shown under a *different* legal name (franchise, client list) is not accepted.
2. **Registry-declared homepage showing the exact legal name** (for example `JAKT OG FJELLSPORT AS`). Not accepted on name alone when the page names other legal entities (`... AS`) or uses portfolio/group/customer-list wording, because that is the shape of a parent's, investor's or supplier's site.
3. **Domain spelling the exact legal name, with the exact legal name on the page** (`kongsberg-regnskapstjenester.no` showing `KONGSBERG REGNSKAPSTJENESTER AS`), under the same risky-wording abstentions as tier 2.

Removed in November 2026: the old tier 3 accepted a registry-declared homepage whose domain spelled the legal name even when the page showed neither the name nor the number. Such a page is now withheld with the reason "Domain spells the legal name but the page shows neither the exact legal name nor the organisation number". This followed organiser feedback that two published sites belonged to a group or parent. A same-host page that names a different legal entity (a sibling, parent or agency) does not inherit the host's proof. `scripts/export_snapshot.py` re-applies the current rule to older envelopes before anything is published to the website.

Name-derived domains (`www.<legal-name>.no/.com`, tried when the registry lists no website or its listed website fails verification; the reason each candidate failed is kept in the candidate ledger) are discovery candidates. DNS is checked first, so nonexistent guesses cost no HTTP request. They publish only through tier 1 or tier 3. Inconclusive home pages may lead to two same-host contact/about/privacy pages, each gated independently. When one of them proves the host, the home page's own meta description is cited from the home snapshot, naming the proving page in its identity basis, unless the home page shows another identifier or legal name or is parked.

A legal suffix that is also an ordinary word (AS, SA, DA, BA) must be written in upper case and must not be followed by another all-capitals word: "Greystone as a result", "Example Assistance" and "FOLLOW AMALIE AS WE TRAVEL" are not the legal names GREYSTONE AS, EXAMPLE AS or AMALIE AS.

After a host is verified, up to five observed same-host pages (careers, news, contact, about) are read. A page on the verified host inherits host-level ownership only. It never adds the home description again, and any page carrying another organisation number publishes nothing. From verified pages AviScope publishes: `og:site_name` brand, linked company profiles on known platforms (link URLs only; Facebook/LinkedIn/Instagram/X/YouTube/TikTok are never fetched; share/intent/login/dialog/person links are rejected; Facebook's `/p/<Page-Name>-<id>` page form is accepted; the handle must spell the legal name, brand or domain; one per platform), JSON-LD `JobPosting` (rejected when `hiringOrganization` names a different taxID or a different name) and JSON-LD articles with headline and publication date. The registry's own `hjemmeside` value is also kept as the official `registered_homepage` fact.

Adversarial tests cover name-only matches, franchise names, parent/shared identifiers, portfolio mentions, parked pages and numbers assembled from unrelated text. Compared with the starter's name-token threshold, these cases exercise stricter rejection, not independent quality measurement.

Identity links contain endpoints/domain, relationship type, status, source snapshot/locator or evidence IDs, and reasons. Rejected candidates stay in `candidate_ledger`. Roles and locations never get inferred from a brand's group site. Unresolved same-period/scope values enter `review_queue`; different periods and group/entity scopes are separate keys.

September 14 update: an inconclusive home page with no conflicting organisation identifiers may lead to at most two observed same-host contact links. Each page must independently pass the same exact-entity gate; contact-page proof never authorizes home-page claims. Subsequent pages no longer create conflicting official-website URLs. Structured claims have precise JSON-LD array/graph locators; the integrity auditor now checks HTML organisation spans, descriptions and structured values, including tampering regressions. Live improvement must be measured separately.
